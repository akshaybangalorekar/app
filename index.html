    Solo, and us

    The thing you have never done in front of each other

    Almost every shy couple has this one. It is not a failing — it is just an unopened door, and it is fine to open it slowly.

    Start smaller than you think. It does not begin with performing for each other. It begins with talking about it — and you can do that by typing, in the dark, from across the room.

    • First, just say it out loud (or type it): “I have never told you what I think about when I’m alone.” That sentence alone changes the temperature.
    • Then swap the facts. When, how often, what you picture. No judgement, just curiosity.
    • Then the ladder. “Would you want me to know when you do?” → “Would you want me in the room?” → “Would you want to watch?” → “Would you want to do it side by side?” Each rung is a maybe until it isn’t.
    • Nobody has to go first. Write your answer, hand over the phone, let them read it with the light off if that helps.

    The ancients thought this was ordinary, by the way. The Kama Sutra treated self-pleasure as essential to wellbeing, and a man who could pleasure a woman with his hands was considered well-bred for knowing it.

    When you want it more — or less

    Two kinds of desire

    Most couples have one person whose wanting arrives on its own — a spark out of nowhere — and one whose wanting only shows up once things have already started. Neither is broken, and neither means “less love.”

    The spontaneous one feels rejected when the answer is no. The responsive one feels pressured by being asked cold. So both end up hurt by the same moment.

    Think of desire as an accelerator and a brake. Stress, exhaustion, resentment, being touched-out by the kids — those are brakes. Novelty, feeling wanted, unhurried touch, a cleared evening — those are the accelerator. You cannot fix a stuck brake by flooring the accelerator. Take your foot off the brake first.

    • Schedule it. A night you both protect. Anticipation does half the work for the responsive partner.
    • Let “yes” mean “yes to starting” — not yes to finishing. Permission to change your mind is what makes starting safe.
    • Touch without a demand. Back rubs and kisses with no expectation retrain the brake.
    • Deal with resentment first. Unresolved anger is the biggest brake there is.
    • Take sex off the scoreboard. Keeping count poisons it.
    How often is enough

    Ask each other plainly: what would feel good — a week, a fortnight, a month? Then meet nearer the higher number, not the lower. The lower-desire partner sets the ceiling only if you both let them.

    And separate “sex” from “connection.” A couple who touch, kiss and flirt all week has far less riding on any single night.

    Sensate focus

    The classic exercise for rebuilding touch when sex has gone quiet. Three stages — do not rush them.

    1. Touch, nothing elseFifteen minutes each. One lies down, the other touches everywhere except breasts and genitals. No goal, no finishing. Say only what feels good. Then swap.
    2. Add the restSame again, now including breasts and genitals — but still no goal, no rush to orgasm. This is a map-making exercise. Learn their body again.
    3. Let it leadNow you may finish. Stages one and two existed to take the pressure off and make the third one easy.

    It will feel awkward at first. That is normal, and it passes. Persist for a few weeks before deciding it does not work.

    What Seema Anand would tell you

    A London-based mythologist who has spent twenty years reviving the Kama Sutra for modern couples. Her central point is the opposite of what most of us assume.

    Seduction is the main event, not the warm-up

    Most couples treat sex as the point and everything else as the approach. The Kama Sutra has it backwards: “there is only so much one can do with the genitals. The real excitement comes from what happens before and after, from what the mind can conjure up.”

    Which means the work happens in the kitchen, in a text at 3pm, in a look across the room — not in the bedroom.

    Fire and water

    Her favourite image from the Kama Sutra. Men’s desire is like fire: it starts at the genitals, flares fast, and is easy to put out. Women’s desire is like water: it starts in the mind and flows down, takes far longer to come to the boil — and far longer to cool.

    If you two have ever felt mismatched, that is not a fault in either of you. It is the design. The whole art of seduction exists to slow one down and warm the other up.

    The mind is the biggest erogenous zone

    Arousal, in her reading, is “a combination of physical intimacy and mental fantasy.” So talking, imagining, teasing, remembering — all of it is foreplay, and none of it requires you to be brave in bed.

    Variety is the whole secret

    Asked how to keep desire alive, the Kama Sutra’s answer was variety in everything — if it is a kiss, then there are five hundred kinds of kiss, using lips, tongue, teeth, chosen by the occasion.

    The kiss, the place, the time, the mood. Change one small thing and it is new again.

    And seduction is for you

    Her own words: seduction “is not an event — it is not about doing it for your partner... seduction is for yourself, it should be a state of mind. Something that puts a spring in your step, a lilt in your voice and the fun into sex.”

    So it is not a performance you have to be good at. It is a way of carrying yourself — and shy people can do it perfectly well.

    Her practical advice, from the Kama Sutra

    • Leave the phones in the other room. “If there were mobile phones in 300 AD, the Kama Sutra’s first mantra would have been to leave them in the other room.”
    • Talk, joke, play a game first. Not a deep talk — a silly one. It dissolves the day and makes you present.
    • Don’t eat a big meal right before. Leave two hours; the body can only digest or desire. The wait raises the anticipation.
    • The no-touch kiss. Feather-light, one point of contact, tease the lip — don’t grab or rush. “When there is just one point of bodily contact, you savour it more.”
    • Make sounds. “Silence is for monks.” Lovers express arousal through sound — though with a child asleep down the hall, a whispered sound counts.
    • Perfume different parts of the body. Each area a different scent, so you become “a banquet of smells, textures and tastes” that invites them to travel your body.
    • Cushions. Placed under the hips or back they change the angle and the sensation. The Kama Sutra recommended eight shapes.
    • What you do after decides the next time. Cuddle, talk, make them feel secure. “Post-coital intimacy will make up for anything that you don’t do particularly well in bed.”
    • Become interesting to yourself. The Kama Sutra’s sixty-four arts — music, dancing, learning, dressing well. The more you feel like a person worth desiring, the more you feel desirable.

    Sources: Seema Anand, The Arts of Seduction, and her interviews with Hindustan Times, Scroll, Vogue India and Harmony.

    Finding time, with an 11-year-old in the house

    This is the real obstacle, so let’s be practical. At eleven he has a bedtime, goes to school, and is old enough to understand a closed door. That is three gifts.

    • He’s at school. A weekday morning after drop-off, or an afternoon off that you both take. Twenty good minutes is a real window.
    • He has a bedtime. Use the hour after — but don’t let it become the only time, or bedtime starts to mean “the sex alarm” and the pressure kills it.
    • Teach privacy, not secrecy. At eleven, a shut bedroom door is normal and healthy. A simple house rule — “Mum and Dad’s room is private, knock and wait” — models good boundaries. You are not hiding. You are being adults.
    • Get the door to lock. If it doesn’t, a door wedge from the inside. The fear of being walked in on is what kills arousal faster than anything. Remove it and you can both relax.
    • Saturday morning lie-in. Headphones, a film, a friend’s house. “We’re having a lie-in” is a complete sentence — you owe no one an explanation.
    • Swap nights with another family. One Friday a month he sleeps at a friend’s. That is one whole evening a month of an empty house.
    • Lower the bar. It does not have to be a night away. Twenty unhurried minutes beats a perfect evening that never arrives.
    • Protect one night a week. In the calendar, like anything else. Adults with children do not have spontaneous sex — they have planned sex, and that is not a failure, it is how it is done.
    • On the noise problem. A fan, the washing machine, music, a thick door. And remember the Kama Sutra’s point: a whisper is still a sound. You do not have to be silent — you just have to be quiet.
    • Don’t wait to be in the mood. Especially if one of you is the “water” type — start the touching and let the mood arrive. Waiting for the mood is how weeks turn into months.

    Date night, by mood
    Scheduling, the way they do it

    Their most argued-about idea. The short version: spontaneous sex is mostly a myth, and planning it is how adults with a child in the house actually have any.

    • Spontaneous sex is largely a myth. When you were dating you scheduled dates — and sex happened on those dates. You were never spontaneous. You were organised.
    • Pick your type. A standing day. Or a morning check-in: “do we want to today, and when?” Or scan the month and mark the good windows. Or just plan the next one after each one.
    • Put it as early in the evening as you can. 11:30pm is where desire goes to die. “We are not watching TV until we have done this.”
    • Build the anticipation. Do what you did when you were dating: shower, shave, pick something to wear, send a text at 3pm. Count down to it.
    • Lower the bar — agree a bare minimum. Sex does not have to mean penetration. Pick one thing you would both do on any night: naked cuddling, a massage, a make-out, side by side. That is all you are promising. More can happen, but nothing more is required.
    • Take turns leading. One of you plans it and starts it each time. Some couples do “king and queen nights” — one person’s pleasure all evening, then swap next time.
    • Sex first, date second. Have sex before the date night, not after. By the time you are home and full you will not want to.
    • Hate the phrase? Do not call it scheduled sex. Call it date night, or intentional intimacy. Same thing, friendlier name.
    • One honest caveat. Scheduling only works if the sex you are having is good. It is not a fix for bad sex — it is a fix for no sex.

    Source: Vanessa & Xander Marin, Sex Talks, and the Pillow Talks episode “How To Schedule Sex.”
